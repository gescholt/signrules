{\LARGE\bfseries signrules: what changed since our first paper's algorithm}\par\vspace{2pt}
{\small Internal note \quad \url{https://github.com/gescholt/signrules} \quad v0.4, Oct.\ 2026 \quad
numbers from \texttt{docs/SIGNRULES.md}}\par\vspace{3pt}
{\small \textbf{Killed at the root}: a contradiction before any branching. \textbf{Minimal impossible}:
impossible, and every one-species deletion is impossible or trivial.}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\sect{1. Starting point: the Maple algorithm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item the 3-term Grassmann--Plücker relations;
  \item \R{R1}, weak stability: $\det B>0$, and some principal $k$-minor positive for every $k$;
  \item \R{R2n}, Cramer's rule on the full system $Bx=a$;
  \item branching on the open signs.
\end{itemize}

\sect{2. Methods added}
\sub{Rules from CIPZ's arguments}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \R{R2}: Cramer's rule on every subsystem $B_{JJ}x_J=w_J$, not only the full system.
  \item \R{R3}: for balanced patterns ($SBS$ a Z-matrix for a signature $S$), the principal-minor signs of
    an M-matrix.
  \item \R{R5}: Laplace expansion of every minor along a row or a column.
  \item \R{R4} (Z-blocks), \R{R6} (Schur blocks): implemented, then measured to force no sign once the
    other rules run; idle by default.
\end{itemize}
\sub{New rules}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \R{R7}, tope rule: $(+,\operatorname{sign}a)$ must be a tope of $[I\,|\,B^{T}]$ (oriented-matroid
    Farkas lemma). It captures everything feasibility implies at the sign level, and contains R2.
  \item \R{R8}, real eigenvalue: if a signature $S$ and a permutation $\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}$ make
    $SBP_\sigma$ a Z-matrix, and $Sa>0$ (or $Sa$ has exactly one wrong sign and $\sigma$ an even number of
    cycles), the Jacobian has a positive real eigenvalue: impossible.
\end{itemize}
\sub{Search}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Failed-literal probing: if one value of an open sign leads to a contradiction, the other is forced.
  \item Symmetry breaking with the relabellings that fix the pattern (SBDS).
  \item \texttt{decide} (branch on chosen coordinates), \texttt{assume} (exact test whether some completion
    has given signs), \texttt{seed} (a random completion).
\end{itemize}
\sub{Trust}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every kill carries a certificate: only the steps the contradiction depends on.
  \item An independent replayer re-derives each step from its identity: all 76 root kills
    ($11+24+41$) replay; flipping any single step makes the replay fail.
  \item On 75 random feasible, stable systems ($n=3,4$) per test run, the rules never reject the true
    sign table.
\end{itemize}
\sub{Screens}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Root propagation on all 248{,}436 non-trivial five-species patterns.
  \item Extension screens by the deletion lemma: 83 candidates at $n=5$, 143 at $n=6$.
  \item Benefactor permutations, every $n$: $\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}$; species fixed by $\sigma$ grow and
    benefit from everybody, every other $i$ is obligate and benefits only from $\sigma(i)$. All impossible.
\end{itemize}

\sect{3. Optimisations}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.40\columnwidth}@{\hspace{0.7em}}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.55\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
change & measured effect \\
\midrule\addlinespace[1pt]
trigger-driven engine: after a sign is set, only the checks reading it rerun & 5--9$\times$ faster than rerunning everything, on the searches of Meng et al.'s eleven \\
flat sign tables, checks compiled to coordinate numbers & 226 first-completion searches of the $n=5,6$ screens: 97\,s $\to$ 51\,s; $n=7$: 10 $\to$ 5\,ms per node \\
most-constrained branching & ${\approx}7\times$ fewer pruned nodes than first-open (32 kills, $n=4,5$, \texttt{R1,R2n,R5,R6}) \\
conflict-activity branching (VSIDS), default & $n=6$ screen (143 searches, limit 20{,}000): 39{,}062 $\to$ 16{,}034 nodes; slowest 290\,s $\to$ 3.4\,s \\
R4, R6 idle (trees unchanged) & four random $n=7$ patterns, 2{,}000-node searches: ${\approx}2\times$ faster \\
probing at every node (optional) & slowest $n=6$ candidate 20{,}000 $\to$ 117 nodes \\
symmetry breaking (optional) & 24 complete searches of symmetric $n=4$ patterns: 38{,}542 $\to$ 18{,}813 nodes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}\par
No optimisation changes a verdict or the set of completions.

\sect{4. What it decides now}
Root kills of the minimal impossible patterns ($n=4$: Meng et al.'s eleven; $n=5,6$: the screens):\par
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
rules & $n=4$ (11) & $n=5$ (24) & $n=6$ (41) \\
\midrule
R1, R2n (first paper) & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
R1, R2 & 3 & 2 & 2 \\
R1, R7 & 8 & 13 & 21 \\
all but R8 & 11 & 21 & 34 \\
all rules & \textbf{11} & \textbf{24} & \textbf{41} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}\par
$n=5$: 24 minimal impossible patterns, ten beyond the CIPZ theorems. Meng et al.\ bounded the number of
impossible five-species patterns by 30 (Fig.\ S3); the true number is 24. $n=6$: 41. Every other
candidate has a tropical witness of possibility, so both lists are complete.
