{\LARGE\bfseries signrules: what changed since our first paper's algorithm}\par\vspace{3pt}
{\small Sign completion for Lotka--Volterra sign patterns on $\mathrm{Gr}(n,2n)$ \quad
\url{https://github.com/gescholt/signrules} \quad v0.4, Oct.\ 2026}\par\vspace{4pt}

Internal note. The package starts from the sign-completion algorithm of our first paper (its Maple
implementation, ``the Maple algorithm'') and adds the arguments of the stable-coexistence paper and new
ones as propagation rules on the same sign variables. Numbers are from \texttt{docs/SIGNRULES.md}.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\sect{1. The starting point}
The Maple algorithm completes the signs of the Plücker coordinates of $[\operatorname{diag}(a)\,|\,B]$ with
the 3-term Grassmann--Plücker relations, \R{R1} (weak stability: $\det B>0$ and a positive principal
$k$-minor for every $k$) and \R{R2n} (Cramer's rule on the full system), branching on the open signs.
\begin{itemize}
  \item On the eleven four-species candidates it kills S-a, S-b, S-c only after branching
    and leaves the other eight with 64 to 15{,}512 completions.
  \item On the ten new five-species kills it leaves 158--173 signs open at the root and never closes.
\end{itemize}

\sect{2. Methods added}
\textbf{Rules.} \R{R2} Cramer on every subsystem; \R{R3} M-matrix minors of balanced patterns; \R{R5}
Laplace expansions; \R{R7} tope rule, oriented-matroid Farkas (complete for feasibility, subsumes R2);
\R{R8} real eigenvalue of benefactor-permutation patterns. \R{R4} (Z-blocks) and \R{R6} (Schur blocks),
the arguments of the stable-coexistence paper, were implemented and shown subsumed by the others, so they are idle by default.

\textbf{Search.} Failed-literal probing; symmetry breaking with the pattern's stabilizer (SBDS); coarse
cells, exact sign correlations, random completions.

\textbf{Trust.} Every kill carries a certificate (the backward slice of the log). An independent replayer
re-derives each step from the identity it names; flipping any single step makes the replay fail (all
$11+24+41$ kills). The rules never reject a random feasible, stable system.

\textbf{Screens.} All 248{,}436 non-trivial five-species classes at the root; extension screens by the
deletion lemma at $n=5,6$; the benefactor-permutation families, impossible for every $n$.

\sect{3. Optimisations}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.45\columnwidth}@{\hspace{0.8em}}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.49\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
change & effect \\
\midrule\addlinespace[1pt]
trigger-driven engine (propagators re-run only on change) & 5--9$\times$ faster than the full sweep ($n=4$) \\
flat sign tables, propagators compiled to ids & $n=5,6$ screens 97\,s $\to$ 51\,s; $n=7$: 10 $\to$ 5\,ms per node \\
most-constrained branching & ${\approx}7\times$ fewer pruned nodes than first-open (32 kills, $n=4,5$) \\
conflict-activity branching (VSIDS), default & $n=6$ screen 39{,}062 $\to$ 16{,}034 nodes; slowest 290\,s $\to$ 3.4\,s \\
R4, R6 idle (subsumed) & $n=7$ searches ${\approx}2\times$ faster, same trees \\
probing at every node (optional) & hardest $n=6$ candidate 20{,}000 $\to$ 117 nodes \\
symmetry breaking (optional) & nodes halve (38{,}542 $\to$ 18{,}813) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}\par
Verdicts and completion sets never change; only trees and times do.

\sect{4. What it decides now}
Root kills of the minimal impossible patterns:\par
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
rules & $n=4$ (11) & $n=5$ (24) & $n=6$ (41) \\
\midrule
R1, R2n (Maple) & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
R1, R2 & 3 & 2 & 2 \\
R1, R7 & 8 & 13 & 21 \\
all but R8 & 11 & 21 & 34 \\
all rules & \textbf{11} & \textbf{24} & \textbf{41} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}\par
At $n=5$: 24 minimal impossible patterns, ten covered by no theorem of the stable-coexistence paper,
sharpening the earlier bound of 30 candidates to 24; at $n=6$: 41. Every other candidate has a tropical witness of possibility
(repository \texttt{feasible\_ecologies}), so the lists are complete.

\sect{5. Software}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Python (numpy; rich, textual) instead of Maple; MIT licence; 237 tests.
  \item \texttt{signrules} opens a full-screen app: pattern library, sign-matrix editor with the
    interaction network in the article's style, rules and node limit, live search dashboard, verdict with
    certificate and replay. Subcommands do the same for scripts.
  \item Drawings of certificates, search trees and networks; an HTML tree viewer; the data of every
    screen ships with the package.
\end{itemize}
